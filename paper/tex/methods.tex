\subsection{What was already on disk}

The evaluation answers predate this study: the questions, the retrieval runs
and the gradings are taken from records written by runs that finished before
this analysis began. Every quantity below comes from those records or from
later checks that read the same rows: a closed-form identity, a count of the
three nested string rules on all \Answers{} answers, two local
instruction-model judges scoring the stored strings, and a simulation of
four-cell tables. Each record is tied to the manuscript by an
evidence-manifest binding; the analysis code verifies each bound file before
reading it and stops the build on any mismatch.

The evaluation being re-read is small and simple. A question set of
\Questions{} questions was answered by a \Model{} instruction model at
temperature \Temperature{} under \ConditionsWord{} retrieval conditions --- no
retrieval, retrieval over a text-only knowledge graph, and retrieval over the
same graph with image-derived attributes attached --- and each answer was
graded right or wrong against a gold string. The latter two are the
graph-structured form of retrieval-augmented
generation~\cite{lewis2020retrieval,karpukhin2020dpr,guu2020realm,
izacard2021fid,izacard2022atlas,edge2024local} and differ only in whether
the graph carries attributes derived from images. Two such question sets exist, one
built around widely documented entities and one around less documented ones,
giving \Answers{} graded answers in total. Each set is split by a recorded
per-question flag into the \SubsetN{} questions whose answer depends on the
image and the \TextOnlyN{} that can be answered from text alone.

The design is paired: the same question is answered under every condition, so a
contrast between two conditions is decided entirely by the questions the two
conditions disagree on. Everything in this paper follows from that.

\subsection{The four counts a contrast reduces to}

Fix two conditions, an arm and a reference, evaluated on the same $n$ questions.
Each question falls into exactly one of four cells, and we write
\begin{equation}
  (a, b, c, d), \qquad a + b + c + d = n,
\end{equation}
for the number of questions both conditions answered correctly, the arm alone,
the reference alone, and neither. Write $m = b + c$ for the discordant count.
The paired difference in accuracy is $\delta = (b - c)/n$, reported below in
percentage points, and the exact two-sided test is a binomial test at one half
on the discordant pairs, with $b$ successes out of $m$~\cite{mcnemar1947note}.
Write $p(b, c)$ for its p-value, with $p(0,0) = 1$.

Both quantities a contrast can report are therefore functions of the discordant
pair $(b, c)$ alone: $\delta$ is, by inspection, and so is $p$. The concordant
cells $a$ and $d$ enter only through $n$. This is the structural fact the rest
of the method rests on.

\begin{definition}[Grading change]\label{def:flip}
A \emph{grading change}, or flip, is the decision that one answer, under one
condition, on one question, should have been marked the other way. It moves that
question from one cell to an adjacent one and leaves every other question where
it was.
\end{definition}

There are eight such moves, since either condition's grading may change on a
question in any of the four cells, and all eight are available whenever their
source cell is occupied.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projection}
Every flip changes exactly one of $b$ and $c$ by exactly one, and leaves the
other unchanged.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The eight moves fall into four pairs. Changing the arm's grading on a question
in $a$, or on a question in $d$, sends it to $c$ or to $b$ respectively;
changing the arm's grading on a question already in $b$ removes it from $b$,
whether it lands in $a$ or in $d$. The reference's four moves are the mirror
image. In every case the discordant pair moves by one unit in one coordinate.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} is what makes the question exact rather than
approximate. The observed table is a point in the plane of discordant counts, a
flip is a unit step in that plane, and asking how far a conclusion is from being
overturned is asking for a distance.

\subsection{The discordant plane, and the distance on it}

Write $T_n = \{(b, c) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : b \geq 0,\ c \geq 0,\ b + c \leq n\}$
for the triangle of discordant pairs a table of $n$ questions can present, and
$\|\cdot\|_1$ for the $\ell_1$ norm on it. Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} says that
flips project onto unit steps in $T_n$; the next lemma says nothing else is
lost in the projection, so a distance in the plane is a count of gradings.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:metric}
For $n\geq 1$, from a table with discordant pair $(b, c)$, a pair $(b', c')$ is reachable in
$k$ flips if and only if $(b', c') \in T_n$ and
$k \geq \|(b, c) - (b', c')\|_1$, with $k$ of the same parity as that norm.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For necessity, Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} makes each flip a unit step, so $k$
steps move the pair by at most $k$ in $\ell_1$ and by exactly that parity, and
$b' + c' \leq n$ because the discordant count never exceeds the number of
questions.

For sufficiency, note first which steps are available. Decreasing $b$ requires
$b \geq 1$ and is achieved by regrading a question in the $b$ cell, either way;
increasing $b$ requires a question in $a$ or in $d$, that is $a + d \geq 1$,
which is $b + c \leq n - 1$; and the two statements for $c$ are the mirror
image. So the available steps at a point of $T_n$ are exactly the unit steps
that stay inside $T_n$, and the choice between the two moves realising a given
step is free.

Now take the decreases first and the increases afterwards. The decreasing phase
lowers $b + c$, so it stays in $T_n$; the increasing phase raises $b + c$
monotonically to $b' + c' \leq n$, so it does too. Because $n\geq1$, every
state in $T_n$ has an adjacent state. The path has length
$\|(b, c) - (b', c')\|_1$, and any two further flips can be spent moving one
coordinate out and back, which supplies every larger $k$ of the same parity.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Minimum flip set]\label{def:mfs}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a claim that is either true or false of a table, and let
$F(\mathcal{C}) \subseteq T_n$ be the set of discordant pairs at which it fails.
The \emph{minimum flip set} of $\mathcal{C}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mfs}
  \kappa(\mathcal{C}) \;=\; \min_{(b', c') \in F(\mathcal{C})}
    \bigl\| (b, c) - (b', c') \bigr\|_1,
\end{equation}
the fewest grading changes after which $\mathcal{C}$ no longer holds, and
$\infty$ where $F(\mathcal{C})$ is empty.
\end{definition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:mfs} is a minimisation over a set rather than a walk, which
is what Lemma~\ref{lem:metric} buys: it replaces reachability by geometry. Both
claims this paper audits are half-planes or unions of half-planes in $T_n$, so
both minima have closed forms.
When the failure set is empty under the fixed design, the value is reported as
$\infty$ rather than as missing; for example, a design with too few discordant
opportunities cannot reach a significant verdict at the registered threshold.

The scope of every minimum below is finite-state. Each is conditional on the
fixed question rows, their binary gradings, the paired sample size $n$, and the
recorded test rule, and it counts how many of those grading decisions would
have to change. It is a count over a fixed table rather than a sampling
distribution, a confidence bound, or an estimate for unobserved questions.
When the text asks what the test ``could have returned'', it varies the binary
table within $T_n$ while holding the design and the decision rule fixed.

\subsection{Two conclusions per contrast, and two distances}

A contrast can carry two different claims, and they are unseated by different
things. The first is a direction: which condition is ahead. The second is a
verdict: whether an exact test calls the difference significant at the
conventional threshold of \AlphaLevel{}. This study measures the distance to
each separately, because its central observation is that they are frequently not
the same distance.

The direction is unseated when the difference reaches zero or reverses. The
verdict is unseated when the p-value crosses the threshold, in whichever
direction it currently sits: a significant result is unseated by becoming
non-significant, and a non-significant one by becoming significant. The second
half of that is not symmetry for its own sake. A verdict of ``no effect'' is
reported and acted on exactly as a verdict of ``effect'' is, so it is entitled
to the same audit.

\begin{proposition}[The direction]\label{prop:direction}
The minimum flip set of the claim that the arm leads is
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_{\mathrm{dir}} \;=\; |b - c| \;=\; n\,|\delta|,
\end{equation}
attained by regrading $|b - c|$ questions in the leading discordant cell.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $b > c$; the other case is symmetric and $b = c$ leaves nothing to
unseat. The claim fails at any $(b', c') \in T_n$ with $b' \leq c'$. For such a
pair,
\begin{equation}
  |b - b'| + |c - c'| \;\geq\; (b - b') + (c' - c) \;=\; (b - c) + (c' - b')
  \;\geq\; b - c ,
\end{equation}
so no target is closer than $b - c$. The pair $(c, c)$ attains it: it lies in
$T_n$ because $2c \leq b + c \leq n$, and its distance is exactly $b - c$. The
second equality is the definition of $\delta$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:direction} is worth stating plainly. The distance from a
reported direction to its own reversal is the numerator of the difference the
evaluation already reported, which no results table currently prints because it
prints the ratio instead. A reader who is shown a difference of \HarmDiff{} percentage points on
\SubsetN{} questions is being shown a lead of one graded answer, and the
conversion is division.

\subsection{The verdict, and what the search costs}

The verdict has no comparably short expression, because the significant region
of $T_n$ is not a half-plane, and that asymmetry is the reason the two distances
come apart. It is still available without any walk over tables. Write
\begin{equation}
  w_\alpha(m) \;=\; \min\bigl\{ w \in \{\lceil m/2 \rceil, \dots, m\} :
    p(w, m - w) \leq \alpha \bigr\}
\end{equation}
for the smallest majority among $m$ discordant pairs that the test calls
significant, undefined where no split of $m$ pairs reaches $\alpha$.

\begin{proposition}[The verdict]\label{prop:verdict}
For each target discordant count $m'$, the pairs of $T_n$ with $b' + c' = m'$
that the test calls significant are those with $b' \geq w_\alpha(m')$ or
$b' \leq m' - w_\alpha(m')$, and the remainder are those it does not. The
distance from $(b, c)$ to any such set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verdict-scan}
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ver}} \;=\; \min_{0 \leq m' \leq n}\ \min_{b' \in A(m')}
    \bigl( |b - b'| + |c - m' + b'| \bigr),
\end{equation}
where $A(m')$ is the admissible interval or pair of rays just described. The
inner minimum is attained at whichever endpoint of $[\min(b, m'-c),
\max(b, m'-c)]$ survives clamping to $A(m')$, so it costs a constant number of
operations once $w_\alpha(m')$ is known.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first sentence is the monotonicity of the binomial tail: at fixed $m'$ the
p-value is decreasing in the majority $\max(b', m' - b')$, so the significant
splits are exactly those whose majority reaches $w_\alpha(m')$, on either side.
Equation~\eqref{eq:verdict-scan} is then Definition~\ref{def:mfs} with the
target set decomposed by $m'$. For the inner minimum, write $u = b'$ and observe
that $|b - u| + |u - (m' - c)|$ is convex and piecewise linear in $u$, equal to
$|m - m'|$ on the interval between $b$ and $m' - c$ and rising by one per unit
outside it. A convex piecewise linear function restricted to an interval or to a
ray is minimised at the projection of its own minimiser onto that set.
\end{proof}

Three costs are worth separating, because the gap between them is what the two
lemmas are for. A breadth-first walk over four-cell tables, which is the direct
reading of Definition~\ref{def:flip}, visits $\Theta(n^3)$ states, since a state
is four non-negative counts summing to $n$. Lemma~\ref{lem:projection} collapses
$a$ and $d$ and leaves $\Theta(n^2)$ lattice points.
Proposition~\ref{prop:verdict} reduces it further to one pass over $m'$, and
because $w_\alpha$ is non-decreasing in $m$ and rises by at most one at a time,
a single scan tabulates every value of it: the whole computation is $\Theta(n)$
evaluations of the test. Proposition~\ref{prop:direction} needs none at all.
Both monotonicity properties are asserted as checks in the analysis code rather
than assumed, so a threshold for which they failed would stop the build.

\begin{corollary}[When no answer could have helped]\label{cor:floor}
A paired comparison on $n$ questions can return a significant result at level
$\alpha$ only if $n \geq \lceil \log_2 (2/\alpha) \rceil$, which is
\SignificanceNeedsM{} at $\alpha = \AlphaLevel{}$, and only if its discordant
count reaches the same bound.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The smallest p-value available on $m$ discordant pairs is $\min(1, 2^{1-m})$,
returned when every pair falls the same way, and $m \leq n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{How the reported numbers were produced}

The values in this paper come from the two-index closed form of
Propositions~\ref{prop:direction} and~\ref{prop:verdict}. The form is exact on
every four-cell table: a breadth-first walk over the eight adjacent moves agrees
with it on all \ClosedFormStates{} states of the three designs
(\ClosedFormMismatches{} mismatches). The search is retained as a unit test
rather than as the definition.

Both distances are recomputed from the propositions for every one of the
\Contrasts{} contrasts, and the build stops if either disagrees with the search
by a single grading. The two procedures share no code and reach the answer by
different routes --- one enumerates tables, the other measures a distance --- so
the derivation above is checked on every build rather than asserted here.

Where the minimum is one, the paper enumerates every individual grading that
would suffice on its own, so a reader can see which answers carry a conclusion
rather than being told how many. Several candidates do not make a conclusion
sturdier: it means there are several answers, any one of which carries it alone.

\subsection{What the test could have returned}

An exact test on paired binary data is a binomial test at one half on the
discordant pairs alone~\cite{mcnemar1947note}, which is the standard choice for
comparing two systems on a shared test set~\cite{dror2018hitchhiker}, so the
smallest p-value it can
return is fixed by the number of discordant pairs before any answer is graded.
That floor is reported for every contrast. A contrast whose floor lies above the
threshold could not have been called significant however the answers came out,
and its non-significance is therefore a fact about the question set rather than
about the conditions being compared.

Intervals are exact 95\% intervals conditional on the observed discordant count:
they use Clopper--Pearson on the discordant split and map it onto the paired
difference~\cite{clopper1934use}, matching the construction the records already
carry. Where a contrast has no discordant pairs the test is undefined and its
p-value is reported as one rather than as an error. In that $m=0$ case the
discordant-split sample is empty: the generated zero-width marker $[0,0]$ is a
conditional bookkeeping statement about the observed table, not an
unconditional 95\% interval for a population paired difference.

The effect forest is only a second encoding of these same quantities. It uses
the recorded percentage-point difference as the point, the mapped interval as
the horizontal bar, and the discordant count as $m$; it introduces no pooled
model, rank ordering, or additional endpoint.

\subsection{Deletion, as a separate question}

Beside each flip distance the paper reports a leave-one-out
count~\cite{efron1979bootstrap}: how many of the $n$ single-question deletions
leave the direction, and separately the verdict, as it was. This measures
something different. A flip asks what a different grading of the same question
set would have done; a deletion asks what a different question set would have
done. A conclusion can be robust to one and fragile to the other, and both are
reported for every contrast rather than one standing in for the other.

\subsection{Multiplicity}

The records report \Contrasts{} paired contrasts and state that their p-values
are unadjusted. This paper adds the adjustment the record names as the one that
would raise the bar~\cite{holm1979simple} and reports it beside each raw value.
The adjustment is applied to the whole family of \Contrasts{}, because that is
the family that was computed; no subset of it is treated as though it had been
specified in advance.

\subsection{Checks the build enforces}

Several sentences in this paper are enforced as build-time checks rather than
asserted in prose, and the analysis code stops rather than reporting a number
that would contradict them.

Every one of the \Contrasts{} contrasts is recomputed from the per-answer rows
and checked against the recorded version on three quantities separately: the
number of questions, the difference in accuracy, and the exact p-value. The
discordant split is checked as well as the difference, because a table matching
on the difference alone could produce the same p-value by coincidence. The
recorded verdict is checked to follow from the recorded test rather than being
taken on its word.

The reshaping from per-answer rows into paired tables refuses a question that
was not answered under every condition, refuses a condition that answers the
same question twice, and refuses a question whose subset flag differs between
conditions --- each of which would let a pair be formed across the wrong things.
Each run's own summary is checked to still restate the rows in its own
directory, and the fixed measurement table the project recorded as its result is
checked to still come from those rows.

The regrade record is checked to contain exactly one contested grading in the
declared image subset and condition. A later count of the same three nested
rules over all \Answers{} answers is checked to contain \RegradeContested{}
disagreements, and the numeric-tolerant rule is checked to move the long-tail
text pair's Holm-adjusted p-value above \AlphaLevel{} in the family of
\Contrasts{}. The publication reports those aggregate counts; question keys,
answer strings, and gold values remain in the private evidence. The value the project recorded as not to be used is checked to still
be recorded that way. The comparisons reported here as absent or incomplete are
checked to still be in that state, the blocked component is checked to still be
blocked with no score substituted for it, and the retrieval result is checked to
still carry the index state that keeps it a footnote.

\subsection{Scope of the computation}

The computation stops at the records. No evaluation answer is regenerated, and
the grading of the original comparison is left as the records made it: the
contested nested-rule disagreements are ones the records already contain,
applied as the records state them rather than as a judgement made by this
paper. Two local instruction-model judges later scored those same stored
strings and wrote no new answers. The reference benchmark this line of work
would eventually be measured against was never downloaded; that comparison is
recorded as \PrimaryTier{}, the comparison against the strongest published
method that would follow it as \SotaTier{}, and no value is imputed for either.
The \BlockedComponent{} component is recorded as blocked on a service that was
unreachable, and its scores \ForgedState{} invented in its place. Each of those
states is carried into the manuscript as recorded.
