Small paired evaluations turn a handful of graded answers into a conclusion.
We analyze one completed evaluation of \Questions{} questions
under \ConditionsWord{} retrieval conditions, with \Answers{} graded answers
and \Contrasts{} recorded contrasts, and ask how few gradings
would have to change for each conclusion to stop holding.
One grading moves a question between the four paired counts, so the minimum
is exact.

For the image-dependent harm contrast, \HarmK{} grading is sufficient:
\HarmCandidates{} of its \HarmGradings{} gradings would each erase
the difference. Three nested string rules disagree on
\RegradeContested{} of \Answers{} answers, one of them on this contrast,
which the numeric-tolerant rule moves from \HarmDiff{} to \RegradedDiff{}
points. The help contrast is \HelpDiff{} points the other way
and needs \HelpK{} gradings to reverse, but its non-significant verdict needs
only \HelpVerdictK{} (\(p=\HelpP{}\)). Under the stored rule \SurvivesHolm{}
of \Contrasts{} contrasts survive Holm correction, two of them the same
long-tail text table counted twice; under the numeric-tolerant rule that
table does not (\(p=\NumericHolmLT{}\)). For \Incapable{} contrasts the
smallest attainable \(p\) exceeds \AlphaLevel{}.

The contribution is an exact minimum-change count for paired evaluations,
taken separately for direction and verdict, and a grader-conditioned reading
of one evaluation's conclusions.
