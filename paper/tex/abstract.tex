Small paired evaluations turn a handful of graded answers into a conclusion.
We analyze one completed question-answering evaluation of \Questions{} questions
under \ConditionsWord{} retrieval conditions, with \Answers{} graded answers
and \Contrasts{} recorded contrasts, and ask for each contrast how few
individual gradings would have to change for its conclusion to stop holding.
Exhaustive search gives an exact minimum because one grading moves a question
between the four paired counts.

For the image-dependent harm contrast, \HarmK{} grading is sufficient:
\HarmCandidates{} of its \HarmGradings{} individual gradings would each erase
the observed difference. An independent regrade of the same answers finds
\RegradeContested{} disagreement; applying it changes the difference from
\HarmDiff{} to \RegradedDiff{} percentage points. The help contrast is
\HelpDiff{} percentage points in the other direction and needs \HelpK{} gradings
to reverse, but its non-significant verdict needs only \HelpVerdictK{}; the
direction and verdict therefore differ by a factor of \HelpRatio{} (the
observed verdict has \(p=\HelpP{}\)). The only contrast surviving correction
for all \Contrasts{} comparisons needs \SigK{} gradings. For
\Incapable{} contrasts the smallest attainable \(p\)-value already exceeds
\AlphaLevel{}, and \AtFloor{} return the most extreme attainable value.

We do not adjudicate whether retrieval conditions differ. The contribution is
an application of a minimum-change perturbation count to annotation-limited
evaluation, read alongside established fragility and resampling measures; it
is not a new statistical family or a mechanism claim. The results are one
evaluation and require validation on a broader set of paired comparisons.
